# Write your first short story

Start from the dashboard home, set up your outline and character sheet, draft Chapter 1, and polish and export your manuscript.

## 1. Open or create your first work

On the dashboard home, choose the starting path that fits what you have ready:

- **Choose a format and start writing**: Above the creation box, pick **Long-form Novel** (default folders for **World Building**, **Characters**, **Materials**, **Outlines**, and **Drafts**), **Short Story** (default folders for **Characters**, **Concept**, **Materials**, and **Drafts**), or **Short-drama Script** (default folders for **Characters**, **World Building**, **Materials**, **Episode Outlines**, and **Scripts**). Type a brief premise into the box (for example, *"Urban mystery: an amnesiac medical examiner finds his own signature inside a sealed cold-case file"*) and click **Start Writing** to open the work and send your premise straight to the AI assistant. Leaving the box blank and clicking **Start Writing** opens an empty project workspace. On a new account, you can also click **Open it** directly on the **Your first work is ready** card at the top.
- **Import an existing manuscript (`.txt` / `.md`)**: If you already have chapters written, select your project format and click **Have a draft already? Import it (txt / md)** below the creation box. ZenStory creates a project named after your file, splits recognizable chapter headings (`Chapter 1`, `第一章`, etc.) into chapter files without calling the AI or using your daily AI messages, and opens Chapter 1.

## 2. Build your outline and character sheet

When you open an empty project, the AI assistant on the right offers starter cards. You can lay down an outline first or jump straight into prose:

1. **Generate an outline and protagonist profile**: Click a starter card or describe your premise in chat to have the AI draft a story outline, core conflict, and character sheet.
2. **Inspect and refine setting files**: Whenever the AI writes a file, a file card appears in the reply showing the characters added or changed. Click the card to open that file in the center editor, where you can tweak names, motivations, or world rules directly. The editor auto-saves 3 seconds after you stop typing (or press `Ctrl/Cmd + S` to save immediately).
3. **Move to Chapter 1**: Once your project has an outline, character, or worldbuilding file but no chapter text yet, a **Write Chapter 1** card appears above the chat input so you can start drafting Chapter 1 from your established settings with one click (or click **Not now** to dismiss it).

## 3. Draft Chapter 1

1. **Choose a generation mode**: In the bottom-left corner of the chat input, pick **Fast** (default; skips automatic post-draft review for faster turnaround) or **Quality** (automatically reviews and revises the draft when needed, which takes longer).
2. **Send your chapter prompt**: Tell the AI the opening scene, point of view, and target length (for example, *"Write Chapter 1 from the outline, opening with a power outage in the autopsy room during a rainstorm, in close third-person POV, around 2,500 words"*).
3. **Steer or stop mid-run**: While the AI is generating, you can type a follow-up note into the input box and click **Add request** to steer the current run without stopping it first, or click **Stop generating** in the bottom right to halt immediately.

## 4. Polish, review history, and export

1. **Quote a passage for targeted edits**: Highlight any passage in the editor and click **Quote** (`Ctrl/Cmd + Shift + Q`) in the floating toolbar to pin it above the chat input, then tell the AI how to revise it.
2. **Run Humanize on stiff phrasing**: Select up to 6,000 characters in the editor and click **Humanize** (`Ctrl/Cmd + Shift + R`) in the bottom status bar. In the split review screen, click `✓` to accept or `✗` to reject individual changes, then click **Finish Review** to save the result.
3. **Browse and restore file versions**: Saved body changes and AI file edits are recorded in **History**; consecutive saves within a short window may be merged into a single version. Click **History** in the editor status bar to inspect version timestamps, source badges, total word counts, and added/removed line counts, click any two versions to select them and click **Compare** to see a diff, or click **Restore this version** to roll back.
4. **Export a `.txt` backup**: Click **Export whole work (TXT)** (or **Export whole script (TXT)**) in the top bar to download the full compiled manuscript, or open **More Actions → Download current file (TXT)** to download only the active file.
