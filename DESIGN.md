Status: Active. Reviewed: 2026-10-17 (in-app announcement and subscription implementations; local verification, not deployed). ## Brand
An open-source story studio that reads as edited and credible. Every organization page shares the homepage tokens: paper `#f5f1e7`, ink `#0a0f1f`, cyan `#22D3EE` for small star accents, and teal `#0d6b7c` (dark `#67e3f5`) for accent text and link hover. Headings, primary actions and the footer use the ink/paper roles. Dark scheme maps the same roles. Reuse `public/brand/zenstory-ai-mark.svg`; social preview is `public/brand/og-zenstory-ai.png` (1200×630). No stock imagery, gradients, badges, testimonials or invented metrics.
## Product goals
Bring the organization homepage’s design, product demonstration and knowledge structure up to the presentation quality of Hypit, while keeping ZenStory’s own brand and factual story-creation scope. Real results appear before a catalog of tools. A visitor can see a demo, understand its input/output, choose a task and follow a practical method. SEO and GEO are acceptance criteria, not promises of ranking or AI citations. No article-count target, invented trust logos, promotional statistics, copied Hypit art or misleading cross-project continuity.
Success signals: real artifact visible at 1440×900 and begins inside 390×844; four task families covered by sourced examples; three input-based paths; four knowledge layers; complete bilingual static HTML; no broken key links or serious/critical axe violations. Search traffic and citation effects require external measurement and are not inferred from screenshots.
## Personas and jobs
Chinese web-fiction authors arriving from an AI answer on a phone; short-drama, game and video creators; English Claude Code users looking for non-coding skills; people who want the browser workbench. They compare formats and install surface before choosing.
## Information architecture
Home → real examples → input-based creative paths → tools and working environments → four knowledge layers → project relationships. Each section answers one question: what a result looks like, what to do with the material you have, which tool and where to run it, where to look something up, how the projects connect. Four skill packs create or adapt; DSH and the browser workbench are separate working surfaces, not extra stages of the same output. The showcase does not repeat the hero's case: five independent example cards cover the Oh Story product video, short-drama sample, finished recap, playable story game and desktop 3D game capture (the playable RPG is the hero stage; its overworld screenshot illustrates the game branch of the manuscript path). They do not represent one story moving through all four tools.
Each example exposes input, output, a same-language method, immutable README/source and a direct playback/prototype link. The hero follows one verified case from source to result — Journey to the West chapter 59 (the public-domain source file), a rule from the Novel to Game source bible, and the playable prototype (browser QA capture plus the game's own character art), all pinned to the owner repository — so a real result is complete inside 1440×900 and visible inside 390×844. The largest showcase demonstrates the writing workflow (poster from 23 s of the real session). Creative tools and working environments have separate visual groups. The homepage carries three action-labeled paths (idea / manuscript / footage) and four layers (get started / whole workflows / techniques and templates / revision and reference). `/guides` retains the complete ten-task taxonomy, optional search and paginated corpus, and reads in the same four layers in order (get started → complete workflows → go deeper by task → revise and look up) with a layer jump row under its search. Its four complete workflows are grouped by starting material (idea; novel → short drama; novel → game; footage); each step page says “workflow · step n/4” at the top and links the next step at the end. Every guide and article opens with the skill its method comes from and its first method files at the pinned commit; workflow steps and list rows name the skill. Search ranks title matches above descriptions, counts topic names only for words nothing else matches, folds in question phrasing and a few writer synonyms, and indexes the glossary terms. A glossary term links the article that teaches it (`how_to` in `glossary.json`). Project pages lead into their craft articles through the project's own complete workflow and task tiles (article count per task, linking the task page) instead of listing every article; task pages and the `/guides` search index list them all. Task pages keep “Start here” with descriptions; below it rows are compact (title and method files), and a task whose articles span at least two skills with three or more each groups them under the skill's reader-facing name (`content/skill-labels.json`, written from each skill's own description) with its pinned SKILL.md, step-by-step guides first and smaller skills under “other methods”. A source labelled only “method used in this article” is shown by its file name. Do not print all articles on home.
Main nav is Projects · Guides · GitHub · EN/中文 · Open app (the glossary left it on 2026-10-09 and is reached from the footer, the homepage's revision-and-reference layer, the end of `/guides` and each term's own article); on home the one primary hero action goes to the creative paths (`#start-h`), the hero caption links the stage case to its own project setup (`/novel-to-game#start-h`) and to the examples, the showcase ends with a pill on the dashed story thread that leads on to the paths, and each section opens with a star-marked eyebrow. Each creative path names the tool it starts with and links to that tool's row; a path the browser workbench can start (the idea path) also ends with one direct signup action for visitors with no agent to install, and the manuscript path's two adaptation tiles show their tool's first showcase result (the same local poster or screenshot); each tool row links to its project page's install-and-entry steps; each knowledge layer's more-link is labelled with where it lands. Existing project, article, glossary, guide and comparison URLs are retained. Workbench docs remain a separate product’s `/docs` corpus, reached from `/workbench` and footer.
## Language delivery
One language per URL. The organization generator runs once per language; `t(en, zh)`, `pick(field)`, `pair()` and `heading()` render the current language only, and `<html lang data-lang>` is set at build time, so nothing depends on a script or on `localStorage`. Each page carries `<link rel="alternate" hreflang>` for en, zh-CN and x-default (English), `og:locale` with its alternate, JSON-LD `inLanguage` for its own language, and the sitemap repeats the pair on both entries. The header switch is a link to the counterpart page; clicking it also sets a `zs_lang` cookie (one year, host-only), and only the organization home reads it back: Vercel redirects a returning visitor from `/` to `/zh` (or `/zh` to `/`) with a temporary redirect when the cookie names the other language. No other URL redirects, there is no Accept-Language guessing, and crawlers, which carry no cookie, always get the page they asked for. Title, description, headline and breadcrumb names are in the page language so Chinese engines match Chinese query vocabulary (网文写作 skill 包, 去AI味, 剪映草稿) in the title itself. On Chinese pages internal links to organization routes are re-pointed at `/zh/...` and per-element `lang="zh-CN"` markers are dropped; English pages keep them on Chinese-only fragments (glossary terms). Organization-host workbench docs and legal pages are exceptions: a single URL with the 中文 article first and the English article below, both anchored, and the switch points at the anchors. App-host legal readers retain the current UI language.
## Design principles
Preserve crawler metadata, language separation, sources and public URL contracts. Homepage headings and layouts may change for the new goal; update obsolete text assertions while strengthening behavior/SEO contracts. Preserve assertions for unrelated page behavior. One type system, one measure, disciplined color. Reduce link density: prose links are ink with a hairline underline; navigational lists and card titles are ink 600 without underline; accent text uses the teal light/dark tokens. Show the same object the same way on every page (one card component, one guide list, one proof chip).
## Visual language
Homepage (`<body data-page="home">`, scoped block at the end of org-pages.css): "manuscript to world". The page is manuscript paper (`#f5f1e7`), the stage and the showcase/closing bands are the ink-navy world, a dashed cream story thread connects things and the mark's cyan star is the only node or bullet. The warm manuscript-paper colour belongs only to paper objects (the hero's source sheet, the novel node in the closing pipe); the three creative-path cards share the card surface in both schemes, the lead path set apart by size and a stronger edge (2026-10-21: a paper-coloured lead card read as a bright block on the dark scheme and a third warm surface on the light one). One family — Geist with the system CJK sans, Geist Mono for paths and commands (loaded only on home via `HOME_FONTS`); the quoted source text is set vertically in a CJK serif. Media share one frame recipe (12px radius, hairline, shadow); a vertical video sits over a blurred copy of its poster, never in a device mock. Rhythm: paper hero → navy showcase → paper tools table / uneven path grid / library → navy closing into the footer. Every other page shares the same tokens, type, header, buttons, star-led eyebrows and radius family (2026-10-10); only the homepage's own layouts (stage, navy bands) stay home-only.
Body and headings: Geist with the system CJK sans stack for both languages, 16px/1.65 (ZH body 1.75), headings 600 with tight negative tracking (h1 clamp 34–52px, h2 26px with 56px top margin and no rule, h3 19px); eyebrows are 12px Geist Mono muted labels led by the cyan star; mono: Geist Mono for paths and commands. One Google Fonts stylesheet (Geist + Geist Mono) loads non-render-blocking (preload + print-swap + noscript) on every page. Measure 720px shared by both languages; page wrap 1120px; 24px gutters; cards 1px borders, 16px radius (boxes, tables and notes 12px), no heavy shadows; buttons 48px tall with 12px radius; page heroes sit on paper with a hairline below, like the homepage hero. Alternating paper/cream bands mark home chapters.

## Accessibility
One `<h1>` per page, ordered headings, `nav[aria-label]`, `main#main`, `lang` on every Chinese node, visible `:focus-visible` on links and buttons (ink ring in light, cyan in dark), AA contrast for text including muted text (#626779) and dark-mode buttons, header controls ≥40px on phones, tables readable on mobile, no motion required. Verify with axe-core on home, project, guide, comparison and glossary pages in both schemes.
## Responsive behavior
Desktop 1440 and phone 390 are the reference widths; 640px and 960px breakpoints. Cards go 3 → 2 → 1 columns; the guide TOC becomes a closed one-row disclosure in flow on phones (≤960px; an inline script opens it as the side rail on wider screens, and without JavaScript it opens on tap); the header stacks to brand + switch + CTA over a link row within 96px down to 360px wide (below 400px the CTA drops its decorative arrow; below 360px the controls wrap to a third row and `--top-h` follows, so anchor jumps always land below the header); nothing scrolls horizontally except code and tables inside their own containers, and — by owner decision on 2026-10-10 — the homepage showcase at ≤720px, where the five cases scroll sideways with scroll-snap (each card 84% wide so the next one peeks in) instead of stacking ~3300px.
## Interaction states
No JavaScript is required to read, see cases or switch language. Videos ship with native controls, no autoplay, preload="none", local posters and always-visible direct-watch fallbacks; with JavaScript one play button stands in for the native bar until the visitor starts playback. GitHub CDN availability is external; posters, descriptions and method links remain usable if playback fails. Recap has a source-derived Chinese captions track; captions for the other videos are not yet established. Guide search progressively enhances a complete static browse index. Only the copy buttons wait for the classic script. Missing paths remain platform 404s. The workbench CTA explains that sign-in may be needed; no token transfer. Links into the workbench (`appHref` in `site-shell.mjs`) carry the page language and the sending slot (`?lang=en|zh&source=org_<slot>`): “Open app” goes to `/login` (a signed-in visitor continues to the dashboard), workbench actions go to `/register`; none lands on the app’s own landing page. The app adopts `lang` and remembers it, and the first `source` in a tab reaches the signup event.
## Content voice
Specific, modest, bilingual. New UI copy is neutral labels or derived from JSON fields (install command, entry command, host list, dates); no ranking, citation, profit or quality guarantees. On home, say a thing once, in the section that owns it, and let the next section pick up from there: the hero caption is one case line plus short actions; hosts, licences and environment rules live in the tool section, project pages and the footer (the home closing carries no star/licence/cost sentence); case cards carry one new sentence each, not their media type again; the tool table shows a short “what you get” note (`home-reading.json` `tools`) rather than the project tagline that restates the goal; the closing keeps the diagram and actions, not the product catalogue again. The same rule holds beyond home (2026-10-10): a guide or article names its project and skill once, in the head; the end keeps only the next step, any method files the head did not show and the install action; a workflow step line at the top gives the place, the next-step link sits at the end; a guide's head points to its own Sources section instead of repeating those links; `/guides` layers skip guides a workflow on the page already lists; project pages list only guides that “How it works” does not already cite; a card or row links a page once (title), with a second action only when it lands elsewhere (install steps). Shorten visible copy, never by shrinking type, lowering contrast or hiding text with CSS (one owner-approved exception, 2026-10-10: the phone-only “swipe to see all N demos” hint above the sideways showcase), and keep crawlable titles, links and no-JS media fallbacks. A novel adapts into drama or a game; a recap starts from footage — copy and diagrams keep that distinction.

## React workbench scope
This section governs `app.zenstory.ai` only; it does not change the organization-site contract above. Evidence: `apps/web/src/index.css`, `components/ui/Modal.tsx`, `ConfirmDialog.tsx`, `Toast.tsx`, `BottomTabs.tsx`, `Layout.tsx`, and `docs/ui-async-state-standard.md`.

- **Goal:** preserve the writing workflow and supported capabilities while fixing demonstrated interaction, accessibility, and state-management defects. Refine visual consistency using the existing primitives and HSL roles.
- **Visual language:** retain the light/dark HSL roles and user accent choices, with white surfaces over a pale neutral canvas in light mode and neutral ink surfaces in dark mode. CSS owns surface/text/border colors; ThemeContext owns the theme class and mode-adjusted user accent. Accent text and pale selected states are separate from the white-on-color primary action. Page and control headings are sans; manuscript typography stays serif. Use fine borders, restrained elevation and the existing icons; the organization site retains its own paper/ink identity.
- **Navigation:** keep dashboard, project editor, materials, skills, billing, settings, and admin boundaries. Inspirations and complex material-analysis stages stay disabled by default; historical data remains readable.
- **Dialogs:** maintain accessible names, initial focus, Tab/Shift+Tab containment, Escape/backdrop policies, scroll locking, and focus restoration. Loading confirmations must not allow cancellation. Nested dialogs must not release the outer dialog's scroll lock.
- **Mobile workspace:** preserve mounted editor/chat/file state across switches. Tabs must reference real labelled panels; inactive panels must be unavailable to keyboard interaction. Retain the existing 768/1024 px layout boundaries and 44 px touch targets.
- **Feedback:** success/info notifications use polite announcements; errors use alert semantics. Keep current deduplication, maximum visible count, and expiry. Loading, empty, error/retry, and disabled states follow the existing async-state standard, not ad-hoc placeholder screens.
- **New build:** after a deploy, an open tab shows one non-modal update card (bottom right, above phone tabs) with 「刷新」 and 「稍后」; it never reloads inside the project editor on its own. Details: `.agents/notes/implemented/feature/2026-10-21-app-update-prompt.md`.
- **Email sign-up:** asks only for email and one password (with a show/hide control); an optional invite code folds behind 「有邀请码？」 (hidden entirely while referral is off) and opens when prefilled, required or rejected; the email field offers common domain completions. Details: `.agents/notes/implemented/feature/2026-10-21-simpler-email-sign-up.md`.
- **Motion:** honor reduced-motion preferences without changing normal animations or removing functional progress indicators.
- **Content:** reuse existing bilingual translation keys, task-oriented labels, and terminology. Do not expose inert actions as working controls.
- **Verification:** regression tests precede behavioral changes; run targeted component/page tests, TypeScript/lint, and keyboard/mobile browser checks. DOM semantics alone do not establish physical focus behavior or visual quality.
- **Open question:** email-change re-verification policy is not established by existing product contracts; preserve it rather than silently introducing a new access policy.

### AI Memory and Writing Conventions

## React workbench buttons and type
